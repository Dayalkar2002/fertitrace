Recommended FERTITRACE Design Principle
Specimen ID (SP000789) should be treated as the primary traceability identifier rather than relying only on the Case/Patient ID. This allows FERTITRACE to maintain traceability as material moves through OPU → Oocyte → Fertilisation → Zygote → Embryo → Biopsy → Cryo → Thaw → Transfer.
Security Note
Avoid putting patient name, mobile number, address, Aadhaar/passport number, diagnosis, medical history, embryo grading, genetic results or other sensitive clinical information directly into the QR. The QR should identify the record; the FERTITRACE database should contain the detailed clinical and traceability information.

FERTITRACE – QR Code Lifecycle & Database Retention
Recommendation: Do NOT delete the QR record after the IVF procedure is completed. Retain the QR identity and complete traceability history in the FERTITRACE database, while changing the lifecycle status as the specimen/container progresses or reaches closure.

Recommended Database Principle
QR Master – stores the permanent QR identity and its lifecycle status.
Specimen/Container Master – stores the biological material and physical container details.
Traceability Event / Audit Log – stores every important scan, witnessing, movement, status change and user action.
Key Rule
Never delete traceability history. When the specimen lifecycle ends, mark the QR as CLOSED, DISPOSED, TRANSFERRED or CANCELLED as applicable. The physical QR may become inactive, but its database record and audit history should remain available according to the applicable record-retention policy.
